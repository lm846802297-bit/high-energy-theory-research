# Source-grounded paper reading

Load this reference for close reading of a paper, explanation of an equation or figure, comparison of versions, or preparation to build on a published result.

## Establish the source

Record the exact source identity:

- DOI or arXiv identifier and version;
- journal/publication version and date, when applicable;
- PDF/HTML/source-TeX identity and retrieval date;
- pages, sections, equation numbers, figure/table numbers, appendices, and supplements actually inspected.

For equations and figures, cite the original paper's own numbering and PDF page. Do not replace a paper's figures with images from another website.

## Read the argument and the evidence

Separate:

1. what the authors explicitly state;
2. what follows from their equations/data under stated assumptions;
3. what is inferred by the reader;
4. what the paper does not report or establish.

Read the relevant methods, appendices, footnotes, figure captions, and supplements when they affect the claim. Compare body text against equations, tables, plots, and supplementary material. Record discrepancies instead of smoothing them away.

For each key figure, identify the claim it is intended to support, the evidence actually displayed, the relevant control/comparison, and any visible counter-evidence or scope limitation.

## Work through important equations

For each key formula:

- preserve the original equation number and source page;
- define symbols, indices, dimensions, units, and conventions;
- explain the role of the formula before manipulating it;
- reconstruct the derivation step by step when the source supports it;
- give a small worked example when it helps expose the operation or physical meaning;
- check limiting cases, dimensions, symmetry properties, and sign/normalization conventions.

Do not invent missing intermediate steps as if they were printed by the authors. Label a reconstruction as a derivation by the reader and state the assumptions used.

For a low-resolution or image-only equation, inspect the original page image. Do not guess unreadable symbols; preserve the image crop and mark uncertain transcription.

## Deliverable

A useful close-reading record contains:

1. the paper's precise central claim;
2. the problem and motivation the work responds to;
3. the key derivation/method and assumptions;
4. the role of the original figures/tables and their PDF page locations;
5. independent checks or reproducibility evidence available;
6. what is established, conditional, not reported, or unresolved;
7. the exact parts of the source inspected.

A close-reading aid is not a substitute for verifying the cited primary source before using it to support a new claim.
